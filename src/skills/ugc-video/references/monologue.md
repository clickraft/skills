# Monologue — what is said in the clip

Write the spoken line before the board, so the board can stage it. It is spoken in one
take by the creator (or, in `product`, by an off-screen voice). Default English, neutral
American accent.

## Word budget

The line must fit the clip with room to breathe. Count words, not characters:

| Clip length | Words (creator formats) | Words (website) |
|---|---|---|
| 4–7 s | 6–12 | 10–17 |
| 8–10 s | 12–20 | 22–26 |
| 11–12 s | 20–28 | 28–33 |
| 13–15 s | 28–35 | 35–40 |

Quiet process videos (an explicit "quiet routine" or "aesthetic" ask) may drop to
≤ 20 words per 15 s. Silent / ASMR shapes drop to zero, or ≤ 15 whispered words.
Tutorials reserve the last 0.5–1 s for a three-word sign-off ("Link in bio.",
"Follow for more.") — shorten an instruction rather than drop a step.

For a part 2+ in a series: no greeting, no re-introducing the product — start
mid-thought.

## Truth contract

- Generated creator → a host or demonstrator script built from what the viewer can see,
  plus exact `approved_claims` strings. No personal history, no "I've used this for
  months".
- User as creator → first person only from their own exact script, confirmed true.
- Productless story → fiction is fine, but no real person, brand or organisation is
  impersonated or made to claim anything.
- No numbers, durations, comparisons, ratings or results unless they are an approved
  claim verbatim.

## Register and persona

Pick the energy register first, then one persona inside it, and write the persona as one
sentence you restate in every later prompt (the voice drifts otherwise):

- **Natural** (default): warm, real, lively — the friend who is genuinely into it. One
  honest peak (a real laugh, a delighted "oh"), never squealing. Under this register the
  persona sentence carries attitude and rhythm only, no loudness words.
- **Hyped** (only when the user says hyped, high-energy, viral energy or similar):
  stretched vowels, big bursts, a squeal at the reveal.
- **Calm** (only on deadpan, minimal, luxury-quiet, moody, clinical signals): flat or soft
  delivery with one small crack of a smile at the peak.

An accent or a quirk only when the brief signals an origin or asks for odd, memorable
energy — offer it in the intake question, never add one unasked. Describe an accent by
its qualities, strongly ("a strong Lagos accent, crisp consonants, sing-song rise"),
never with phonetic spelling.

## Story shape (pick one spine)

Brainstorm three, choose one. Each has exactly ONE turn ("but then") in the middle.

1. Problem shown → feature demonstrated.
2. Opens on a visible action → how it works.
3. A second, unexpected use (shown, not claimed as personal habit).
4. Side-by-side of visible mechanics or approved claims only.
5. Behind the idea — how the product or setup works, no testimonial.
6. Telling a story about something else; the product interrupts it.
7. Getting ready for something that matters; the stakes carry it.
8. New place, mini vlog.
9. Ordinary day with a twist.
10. A clearly staged reveal (no claimed first reaction).
11. Talking to the viewer as a character in a scene.
12. A real task with one small win (good for B2B and small business).
13. Answering a question with visible evidence.
14. Commentary over something shown.
15. A checklist of visible attributes.
16. One direct question, answered.
17. A visible setup change, no invented outcome.
18. Silent flex (no words).
19. ASMR (whisper or none; sounds are the script).

With a product in story shapes, it enters about halfway, as a supporting actor; the
story should still make sense without it. A plain review structure only when the user
asks for "just review it": problem → agitate → solve, or before → bridge → after on
visible states only, or a "watch this" demonstration.

## Hook (the first second)

Pick one opening pattern, never two:

- **Mid-action** — something physical already at its peak in frame one; the first word
  lands during it.
- **Walked-in-on** — the line starts mid-sentence, confessional, close to the lens.
- **Something's off** — one thing in the scene is strange; the voice ignores it.
- **Frozen reaction** — face already reacting, a held beat under 0.7 s, then the line.
- **Call-out** — a challenge aimed at the viewer, slight lean in.
- **Quirk first** — the opted-in quirk is the first event.
- **Mechanism first** — the product is already doing its visible thing; the line names it.
- **Product cold open** — (story shapes, with a product image) a rough product-only
  first shot, then a hard cut to the creator mid-reaction.

## Line craft

- The very first word is content. Never open on: OK, okay, so, alright, yeah so, um,
  well, like, wait, hold on, OMG, hey guys, guys, so basically, story time, stop
  scrolling, let me tell you.
- Banned anywhere: literally, obsessed, game-changer, holy grail, changed my life, hits
  different, elevate, seamless, effortless, revolutionary, "you need this", "trust me",
  ratings like 10/10, generic praise ("amazing", "so good", "unreal").
- Prefer specific sensory and mechanical words: "twists open with one thumb", "sinks in
  without a shine", "smells like pepper and cedar".
- One peak per clip. One or two capitalised volume spikes at most. A single broken
  sentence at the peak is allowed. No scripted dramatic pauses.
- Cut echoes and repeats first — they spend words and say nothing.
- Music only on request; it goes into the clip prompt, never the line.

## Hand-off

Pass on: the exact line (verbatim — the clip prompt never rewords it), the persona
sentence, the register, the hook pattern as one plain staging sentence for slot 1, and
the beat split (which words fall on which slot).
